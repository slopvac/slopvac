If Vincent van Gogh had been a successful artist during his lifetime, several aspects of his life and the broader art world might have been different:

1. **Personal Life and Mental Health**: Success could have positively impacted van Gogh's mental health, although it is difficult to predict to what extent. Financial stability and social recognition might have alleviated some of his stress and depression. This could have led to a longer life, potentially resulting in a larger body of work.

2. **Artistic Development**: With success, van Gogh might have had access to better materials, more diverse environments, and possibly collaborations with other artists. This could have influenced the style, techniques, and subjects of his paintings, possibly leading to different artistic innovations or developments.

3. **Influence on Contemporary Artists**: As a prominent artist, van Gogh would have been in a position to influence his contemporaries more directly. Artists of his time might have been more openly influenced by his techniques or thematic choices, potentially altering the course of post-Impressionism and early modern art.

4. **Art Market and Collectors**: If he had been successful, his works would not have been as obscure during his life. Collectors and museums might have started acquiring his artworks earlier, and his influence on the art market dynamics would have been evident much sooner. This could also mean that his original works would be more widely distributed across the globe in various museums and private collections.

5. **Cultural Perception**: Van Gogh’s legacy as a "tortured artist" might not have been as prominent if he had experienced success and stability. The romanticized narrative of his life might have been less focused on his struggles and more on his artistic achievements and innovations.

6. **Legacy and Museum Exhibitions**: With an established reputation, the focus of posthumous exhibitions might shift. There would likely be more comprehensive retrospectives earlier in the 20th century, influencing how museums and art historians position him within the art historical canon.

Overall, van Gogh's success during his lifetime would have not only transformed his personal circumstances but also had a potentially significant impact on the development and reception of Western art.
